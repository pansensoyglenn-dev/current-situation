<h1>The Problem Is Not Social Media—It Is Irresponsible Use</h1>
<div class="meta-info">
<span>📅 September 25, 2026</span>
<span>📂 Technology, Society, Digital Literacy</span>
<span>🏷️ Responsible tech, Online safety PH</span>
</div>
</header>

<p>The recent controversy involving Discord and Reddit has renewed an important question: Should social media platforms be blamed for the harmful actions of some users? Platforms can certainly be held accountable when they fail to protect children, remove illegal content, or respond to criminal activity. However, the deeper problem is often how people choose to use technology.</p>

<p>Social media is not inherently evil. Facebook, YouTube, Reddit, Discord, and other platforms are tools. Like a knife, a vehicle, or a telephone, they can be used responsibly or irresponsibly. They can connect families, support education, create businesses, and spread useful information. Unfortunately, some users exploit them for scams, gambling, harassment, deception, and other unlawful activities.</p>

<h2>Technology Is Not the Real Enemy</h2>

<p>Facebook allows Filipino families separated by distance to remain connected. Overseas Filipino workers can communicate with their spouses, children, parents, and relatives through messages, video calls, and online groups. Families who might otherwise see one another only once every few years can now share birthdays, celebrations, and everyday experiences.</p>

<p>YouTube can also be an excellent source of knowledge. People can learn farming techniques, cooking, history, language, repair skills, computer programming, business practices, and many other subjects. For a farmer, a useful video may explain how to manage pests, improve soil, operate equipment, or protect crops from severe weather.</p>

<p>The problem is not the existence of these platforms. The problem begins when users deliberately choose harmful or dishonest activities instead of using technology for personal improvement and constructive communication.</p>

<h2>The Growth of Online Abuse</h2>

<p>Some Filipinos use social media to deceive other people. They create fake accounts, pretend to sell products, request money through false emergencies, promote fraudulent investments, or steal personal information. Others use social-media platforms to promote online gambling, manipulate vulnerable users, or spread misleading claims.</p>

<p>These activities harm both individuals and society. Victims may lose their savings, suffer emotional distress, or become suspicious of legitimate online businesses. Honest sellers also suffer because people become afraid to transact online. In this way, the actions of irresponsible users damage the reputation of millions of Filipinos who use technology properly.</p>

<p>The government should enforce existing laws against fraud, identity theft, cyberbullying, exploitation, and illegal gambling. Law enforcement agencies should investigate criminals instead of treating every social-media user as a potential offender. Platforms should cooperate with authorities, but users who knowingly commit crimes must also face appropriate legal consequences.</p>

<h2>Children Need Guidance, Not Only Restrictions</h2>

<p>Protecting young people online is necessary, but a total ban on social media for all young users may not be the best solution. Children can benefit from educational videos, communication with relatives, creative projects, and supervised online communities. A blanket prohibition may also prevent them from developing the digital skills they will need in school and employment.</p>

<p>Parents and guardians have a major responsibility. They should teach children:</p>

<ul>
<li>Never to share passwords, addresses, school details, or private photographs with strangers.</li>
<li>How to recognize fake accounts, suspicious links, false investment offers, and fraudulent online shops.</li>
<li>Why online gambling, dangerous challenges, and illegal activities must be avoided.</li>
<li>How to verify information before sharing it.</li>
<li>How to communicate respectfully and responsibly.</li>
<li>How to report harmful content and seek help when they feel threatened.</li>
</ul>

<p>Parents should also know what platforms their children use, who they interact with, and how much time they spend online. Guidance should not mean secretly humiliating or constantly threatening children. It should involve clear rules, open communication, age-appropriate supervision, and good parental example.</p>

<h2>Better Digital Education</h2>

<p>Schools should include digital citizenship in their lessons. Young people should learn that online actions have real-world consequences and that anonymity does not guarantee freedom from responsibility. They should understand privacy, consent, copyright, misinformation, cybercrime, online etiquette, and the dangers of gambling and financial scams.</p>

<p>The government could also require stronger age protections for services involving gambling, adult content, or financial transactions. However, such measures should be carefully designed so that they protect minors without unnecessarily restricting lawful speech, education, family communication, or access to useful information.</p>

<p>Platforms also have responsibilities. They should improve age-appropriate design, remove clearly illegal content, respond quickly to reports, protect user data, and provide accessible reporting systems in local languages. Yet platform rules cannot replace family guidance, school education, and law enforcement.</p>

<h2>Choosing Productive Uses</h2>

<p>Filipinos can use technology to improve their lives rather than exploit others. A person can watch tutorials to learn a trade, study English, understand agriculture, build a website, market a small business, or develop a creative skill. A young person can use the internet to learn programming, research a school project, or publish artwork instead of spending every free hour on gambling or harmful content.</p>

<p>This does not mean that all entertainment is bad. Gaming, videos, and online discussions can be enjoyable when used in moderation. The concern arises when entertainment becomes addictive, gambling becomes a source of financial harm, or technology becomes a tool for deception.</p>

<div class="highlight">
<p>💡 A shared responsibility: Government must target criminals and require reasonable safeguards. Tech companies must respond to abuse. Parents must teach. Schools must educate. Most importantly, individual users must choose honesty and responsibility.</p>
</div>

<h2>A Shared Responsibility</h2>

<p>The government must target criminals and require platforms to maintain reasonable safeguards. Technology companies must respond to abuse and protect vulnerable users. Parents must teach their children. Schools must provide digital-literacy education. Most importantly, individual users must choose honesty and responsibility.</p>

<p>Social media should not be condemned simply because some people misuse it. Facebook can reunite families, YouTube can educate millions, and online communities can help people share knowledge and opportunities. The real challenge is to build a culture in which Filipinos use technology not to deceive, exploit, or harm others, but to learn, communicate, create, and improve their lives.</p>
