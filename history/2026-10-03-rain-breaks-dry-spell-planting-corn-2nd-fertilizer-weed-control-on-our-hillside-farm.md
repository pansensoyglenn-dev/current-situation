        <h1>The dry spell finally ended. Heavy rain, planting, and the second 14-14-14 application</h1>
        <div class="site-meta">
          <span>📍 <a href="https://current-situation.vercel.app/">current-situation.vercel.app</a></span>
          <span>🌱 Farm journal ·Diplahan, Zamboanga Peninsula</span>

      <p><strong>The dry spell finally ended.</strong> A heavy downpour the night before broke the week-long stretch of dry weather that had left the soil hard and the plants waiting. The rain soaked the parched rows, filled the furrows, and left the morning air cool and sweet.</p>

      <p>With the land refreshed, I brought eight hardworking members of our team into the field to plant the corn. We moved quickly but carefully, opening furrows, placing the seeds at the proper depth, covering them gently with soil, and firming each row so moisture would stay close to the seed. The team worked in rhythm, trading tips and smiling even with damp earth clinging to their boots. I checked spacing and seed placement, made small adjustments for the slightly softer soil, and marked the rows for future irrigation and fertilization.</p>

      <p>Fifteen days after the seeds broke soil, the young maize stood in neat rows under a low damp fog. <strong>Sherwin</strong> rose before dawn and readied the fertilizer, a measured mix of ammonium for quick nitrogen and triple-14 for balanced NPK. He moved between rows with practiced care, applying coarse bands of fertilizer near the root zone to boost leaf growth and root development without scorching tender roots. The mist slowed evaporation, helping nutrients reach roots, so he adjusted spray and pace to avoid leaf residue. He checked plants for yellowing and compacted spots, loosening soil where needed, then logged blends and weather. By mid-morning the fog lifted, the field looked steadier, and Sherwin felt the quiet hope that proper early feeding brings.</p>

      <p><strong>Today marks the second application of triple-14 fertilizer, exactly twenty-five days after the first.</strong> The corn plants now stand tall and vibrant across the sloping field, their broad green leaves catching the soft morning light that filters through the mist. Banana trees rise among the rows, framing the scene with their large fronds against the dense forest backdrop. A worker moves carefully between the plants, tending the soil near scattered rocks and patches of grass. We’re applying the triple-fourteen fertilizer once more to support strong root development and steady growth, ensuring the crop continues to thrive in this lush tropical setting as the sun begins to warm the day.</p>

      <div class="highlight">
        <p><strong>Weed control at second fertilization</strong> — In this lush, rain-soaked hillside field, the weeds are rapidly spreading beneath and between the young corn plants. As we apply the second fertilization, thorough weed eradication becomes essential so the corn can fully absorb the nutrients instead of competing with unwanted growth. Here you can see the worker moving steadily along the narrow path through the dense vegetation, backpack sprayer in place, systematically treating the undergrowth around the corn and remaining banana stools. The misty conditions and thick cover of ferns, grasses, and broadleaf weeds make timely control especially important at this stage of the crop’s development. Clearing the competition now ensures the fertilizer reaches the corn roots efficiently, supporting stronger growth in the weeks ahead.</p>
      </div>

      <p>In the early light of morning, the cornfield is alive with soft greens and the quiet work of the season. Young maize plants stand in neat rows across the sloping ground, their leaves still fresh and upright. Banana trees rise among them, some with older browning leaves hanging down. A large dark rock sits in the middle of the plot, and beyond the crop the misty forest edges the horizon under a pale, rising sun. The weeds that once competed with the maize have begun to wither and die back. Their strength is fading, leaving the soil clearer around the young stalks.</p>

      <p>This is the ideal moment for the second application of fertilizer triple-14, 14-14-14. The balanced NPK blend will feed the growing plants just as they enter a more vigorous stage, supporting stronger roots, greener leaves, and better ear development without the weeds stealing the nutrients. A farmer moves steadily through the rows, carrying a small blue container, working methodically the between the plants. The air is still cool, the light gentle, and the field ready for this next careful step in its care.</p>

      <p>Ten days have passed since the second round of fertilizer was applied to this maize field, and the results are clearly visible across the plots. The corn plants stand tall and vigorous in neat but slightly uneven rows, their broad green leaves unfurling strongly toward the light. Many stalks have reached a promising height, with dense foliage that suggests the nutrient boost is taking effect. While a few plants appear shorter or show yellowing at the tips, the majority display the lush upright growth expected after a timely second fertilizer application. This stage marks a critical period of vegetative development, setting the foundation for stronger tasseling and eventual ear formation if conditions remain favorable.</p>

      <p>To protect the thriving maize field from worm infestations, the insecticide <strong>Yovel</strong> was applied across crop. Lush green corn plants line a muddy pathway as Sherwin, the corn maintenance worker, walks deeper into the field with a blue backpack, surrounded by trees and natural scenery, ensuring healthy growth free from destructive pests.</p>
